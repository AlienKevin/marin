# World-model probe — does full-transcript SFT build a better terminal model?

[Phase 0](/phase-0) measures the **agent** (pass@1). This probe measures the **world model**: how well each checkpoint predicts terminal output it has never been trained to generate. The two questions turn out to point in opposite directions, which is the most interesting result in the project so far.

## Setup

- **Held-out set:** 100 SWE-ZERO trajectories from **100 unseen repos** (zero repo overlap and zero instance-id overlap with the 1M training subset). Sampled from the full 12M-trajectory corpus.
- **Task:** precondition on the first **14 (command, observation) pairs + the 15th command**, then score the perplexity of the **15th observation** (the real terminal output). Intuition: with 14 turns of context the repo state is heavily constrained, so this is the most determined, least-ambiguous version of "predict the next terminal output."
- **Scoring:** teacher-forced HF forward pass, per-token NLL over the target observation only, prompts rendered with each checkpoint's own chat template (byte-identical framing to its SFT). All seven checkpoints score the same 100 targets (33,520 tokens), so only the weights differ.
- **Note:** `vllm-tpu` (our pinned 0.13.x) does **not** implement `prompt_logprobs` — the [feature PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/tpu-inference/pull/2547) is still open — so scoring runs through transformers on CPU, not vLLM.

## Headline

PPL on held-out env tokens (lower = better world model). Two aggregations: **token-weighted** (every target token counts equally — dominated by long outputs) and **median per-trajectory** (the typical observation).

| checkpoint | loss mask | token-weighted PPL | median per-traj PPL | mean | max (worst traj) |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **base** Qwen3-1.7B-Base | none (pretrained) | 2.90 | 1.97 | 4.98 | 33 |
| (a) 10K | assistant-only | 2.01 | 1.81 | 7.21 | 203 |
| (a) 100K | assistant-only | 2.21 | 2.45 | 4.93 | 52 |
| (a) 1M | assistant-only | 2.58 | **3.33** | 14.43 | 325 |
| (b) 10K | full-transcript | 1.74 | 1.17 | 1.54 | 5.1 |
| (b) 100K | full-transcript | 1.79 | 1.11 | 1.55 | 5.3 |
| (b) 1M | full-transcript | 1.87 | **1.07** | 1.59 | 7.9 |
| dedicated simulator (10K) | sim-rewritten | 1.79 | 2.00 | 2.01 | 5.2 |

## Three takeaways

**1. Standard SFT (a) forgets the world model *below the untrained base model*; full-transcript SFT (b) improves on it.** The base anchor makes this sharp. The pretrained Qwen3-1.7B-Base already predicts held-out terminal output at **median PPL 1.97** — it saw plenty of terminal text in pretraining. Against that anchor (median per-traj):

- (a) 10K barely beats base (1.81), then **forgets past it**: 100K → 2.45, 1M → 3.33. So scaled agent SFT makes the model a *worse* terminal predictor than the untrained model it started from.
- (b) beats base immediately and keeps improving: 1.17 → 1.11 → 1.07 (monotone, ~2× better than base by 1M).

The mechanism is catastrophic forgetting: (a)'s loss is on action tokens only, so env-token prediction is protected by nothing and erodes as the model specializes on actions over more optimizer steps — eventually dropping below the base model's incidental competence. (b) keeps env tokens in the loss, so the world model is actively maintained and sharpened. Per-trajectory, 96/100 held-out trajectories degrade under (a) from 10K→1M; (a) also develops a catastrophic tail (mean PPL 7→14, worst trajectory 203→325) that (b) never does (worst stays ~5–8). It is systematic, not outliers.

**2. Pass@1 and world-model PPL point in opposite directions.** From [Phase 0](/phase-0), arm (a) is the slightly *better agent* (e.g. 11 vs 4 / 100 at 1M). Here, arm (b) is the dramatically *better world model* (3× lower median env PPL at 1M). So the two arms make opposite trades: **(a) reallocates capacity to the action policy at the cost of its world model; (b) keeps a usable world model at a modest cost to the policy.** For TerminalWorld's actual goal — a model that can serve as a training environment / be used for model-based RL or planning — this is a stronger argument for ECHO-style unmasking than the pass@1 numbers ever were.

**3. Full-transcript SFT matches or beats a purpose-built simulator, for free.** Arm (b) at 10K equals the dedicated simulator on the token-weighted aggregate (1.74 vs 1.79) and beats it on every per-trajectory statistic (median 1.17 vs 2.00, mean 1.54 vs 2.01, worst 5.1 vs 5.2). So simply not masking env tokens during ordinary agent SFT buys you a terminal model at least as good as one trained explicitly for the job — without arm (c)'s env-substitution/rewrite pipeline. **Caveat:** the two are scored in their *native* framings — (b) with the observation in the `user` role (agent format), the simulator with it in the `assistant` role (sim-rewritten format) — so part of the gap could be framing rather than quality. The clean apples-to-apples test is a **user-only loss mask on the same agent-format data** (see [next steps](#next-steps)); that isolates the mask from the format and would confirm whether the rewrite pipeline buys anything at all.

## Nuance: (b)'s token-weighted PPL rises slightly — why?

The token-weighted column shows (b) ticking up 1.74 → 1.87 even though the median *falls*. This is a length effect, not a broad regression. Bucketing (b)'s per-trajectory change from 10K → 1M by observation length:

| bucket | trajs | share of tokens | median PPL 10K→1M | mean PPL 10K→1M |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| short (≤30 tok) | 39 | 1% | 1.04 → 1.02 | — |
| mid (30–200) | 25 | 7% | 1.22 → 1.13 | — |
| long (>200) | 36 | **92%** | 1.72 → 1.72 | 1.76 → **1.98** |

Short and mid outputs (the typical observation) *improve* with scale. The long bucket holds 92% of all scored tokens, so it determines the token-weighted headline — and within it the median is flat while the mean rises (heavy right tail: the worst trajectory goes 4.35 → 7.85, and ~5 trajectories account for 60% of the entire aggregate increase).

The mechanism is **format generalizes, content memorizes**:

- **Short outputs are format-dominated** — `Observation:` + a couple of grep hits, an exit code, a short listing. Structure is shared across all repos, so more training sharpens it and it transfers to unseen repos. These improve.
- **Long outputs are content-dominated** — a full `cat file.py`, a long `sed` range, a test log: literal repo-specific source. This is only predictable if you've seen *that repo*. On held-out unseen repos there is nothing to ground on, and sharpening content priors toward SWE-ZERO's training repos doesn't transfer. These degrade — but it's a heavy tail of the most idiosyncratic outputs, not a uniform shift.

This is an **inherent ceiling, not an objective failure**: no volume of SWE-ZERO data lets any model emit the literal contents of a file in a repo it has never seen. The only fixes are to put the real content in context (retrieval-grounding, exactly [R-WoM](/literature-review)'s move) or accept a floor on long-output fidelity for novel repos. It is the same root cause as the [Phase 0.5](/phase-0-5) simulator hallucinating plausible-but-wrong file dumps.

## Next steps

The loss mask is the clean axis to push on. Partition every non-system token: **assistant** (commands) vs **user** (task + observations). Then the three arms are an exact decomposition — (a) = assistant-only, (b) = both, and the missing third point **(c) = user-only**:

| arm | loss on | trains a… |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | assistant | pure policy |
| (b) | assistant + user | policy + world model |
| **(c)** | **user** | **pure world model (simulator)** |

with **(a) ⊕ (c) = (b)** exactly. (c) is a one-line chat-template change (the full-transcript template with the assistant markers removed) on the *same* agent-format data — no rewrite pipeline, no sim system prompt. It is the apples-to-apples "pure simulator" this page's takeaway 3 is missing: scored in the same agent framing as (a)/(b), it isolates the mask from the format and tests whether the dedicated simulator's rewrite buys anything. Predicted env-PPL ordering: **(c) ≤ (b) < (a)**. Note (c) is a simulator, so it is graded here (env-PPL), not on SWE-bench pass@1 (which would be ~0 — it never learns to emit commands). The env-substitution / closed-loop work that previously held the "(c)" label moves to its own [Phase 0.5](/phase-0-5) thread.

## Reproduce

Code pinned at commit [`da8d12e`](https://github.com/AlienKevin/marin/tree/da8d12e6b5a79a8186ff75104145c40a28e0ec07) on the `terminalworld` branch.

1. **Build the held-out set** — [`scripts/sample_heldout_unseen_repos.py`](https://github.com/AlienKevin/marin/blob/da8d12e6b5a79a8186ff75104145c40a28e0ec07/scripts/sample_heldout_unseen_repos.py): enumerates the 1M training subset's repos/instance-ids, then samples 100 trajectories (≥15 turns, distinct unseen repos) from the 12M corpus (`seed=42`). Writes `gs://marin-us-east5/heldout/sim_ppl_heldout_100.jsonl`.
2. **Score each checkpoint** — [`scripts/sim_ppl_eval_hf.py`](https://github.com/AlienKevin/marin/blob/da8d12e6b5a79a8186ff75104145c40a28e0ec07/scripts/sim_ppl_eval_hf.py) `--model-path <gcs-or-hf> --mode {agent,sim} [--chat-template-from <ckpt>]`. Teacher-forced HF forward pass on CPU; per-token NLL over the 15th observation only. The base model uses `--mode agent --chat-template-from` an SFT ckpt so prompts render identically.

The scoring **had to** run through transformers because `vllm-tpu` (0.13.x) returns `[None]` for `prompt_logprobs` — confirmed by [`scripts/probe_vllm_logprobs.py`](https://github.com/AlienKevin/marin/blob/da8d12e6b5a79a8186ff75104145c40a28e0ec07/scripts/probe_vllm_logprobs.py); the [feature PR](https://github.com/vllm-project/tpu-inference/pull/2547) is still open. The vLLM and Levanter attempts ([`sim_ppl_eval.py`](https://github.com/AlienKevin/marin/blob/da8d12e6b5a79a8186ff75104145c40a28e0ec07/scripts/sim_ppl_eval.py), [`sim_ppl_eval_levanter.py`](https://github.com/AlienKevin/marin/blob/da8d12e6b5a79a8186ff75104145c40a28e0ec07/scripts/sim_ppl_eval_levanter.py)) are kept to document the dead-ends.

**Artifacts:** held-out set `gs://marin-us-east5/heldout/sim_ppl_heldout_100.jsonl`; per-checkpoint scores `gs://marin-us-east5/sim-ppl-eval/sim_ppl_*.jsonl` (one row per trajectory + a summary row). Checkpoint paths: see the [Phase 0 reproduce table](/phase-0#reproduce).
